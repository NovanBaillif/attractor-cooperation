# Decision for 0.7 — a thin layer over standards that exist, not another vocabulary

Decided by the operator, 22 September 2026, on his own observation: *"I get the impression we are redoing norms
that already exist."* He was right, and our own audit had already said so.

## The measurement that settles it

[`PRIOR-ART.md`](../PRIOR-ART.md), written 17 September after the same objection: fifteen concepts of this
profile examined, **ten already named in published standards** (W3C Web Annotation, PROV-O, nanopublications,
CiTO, FEVER, SLSA VSA, in-toto, OpenTelemetry GenAI, COAR, SARIF), three with no equivalent found, and seven
listed under *what still looks specific* — which that file is careful to call "claims to test, not results".

Two thirds of the vocabulary is therefore other people's work, renamed. Continuing to grow the text would make
this project one more competing dialect, which is exactly what the operator asked it not to be.

## What changes

1. **The profile stops defining terms.** Every member with an equivalent is emitted as that equivalent. The
   mapping table of SPEC section 3.3 becomes the point of the profile rather than an appendix to it.
2. **A kernel stays ours**, because nothing found carries it:
   - a status **derived and never declared** — no participant may write "verified" about itself;
   - a **disagreement that survives a hop**, with the receiver's disposition kept apart from the sender's
     provenance;
   - the **indistinguishability control** of Test #001: a row claiming an independent observation must name an
     observation of the verifier's own that differs between a true and a false world.
3. **The kernel goes upstream, not into our own text.** It is offered where the work already lives: the IETF
   draft of S. Bu (in progress since 18 September), Pramana, and the communities that maintain the vocabularies
   above. Our repository keeps the tests, not the dialect.
4. **Leaving draft status stops being the goal.** The exit criteria of section 10.3 measure whether two people
   can implement our words the same way. They say nothing about whether the words were needed. A profile nobody
   needs can pass them all.

## What this does not throw away

The 134 conformance cases test **rules**, not names: they survive the change and can be re-expressed over the
mapped terms. Test #001, the credit record, the indifference check and the v0.1 re-run measure things no
vocabulary decides. All of it stays.

## First step, already built

[`mapping/to-standards.mjs`](../mapping/to-standards.mjs) emits, from a conformant record:

| Ours | Emitted as | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| `derivation.span` | `Annotation` with a `TextQuoteSelector` and a `TimeState` | W3C Web Annotation Data Model (2017) |
| `derivation.inputs`, `operation`, `channel`, `upstream` | `prov:Entity` with `wasDerivedFrom`, `wasQuotedFrom`, `hadPrimarySource`, `wasGeneratedBy` | W3C PROV-O |
| violations, warnings, derived span values | `runs[].results[]` with `ruleId`, `level`, `message` | OASIS SARIF 2.1.0 |

Five tests check that it is faithful and, as importantly, that it invents nothing: no quote that the record does
not carry, no derivation link the record does not declare, no SARIF result without a matching diagnostic.

## What is not done

- The receipt, the reveal and the replay are not mapped yet; the closest candidates are in-toto attestations and
  the SLSA Verification Summary Attestation, both named in the audit.
- Nothing yet reads these formats back in. Emitting is the easy half.
- The kernel has not been offered upstream in these words. That is the next outward step, and it needs the
  operator's sentence.
