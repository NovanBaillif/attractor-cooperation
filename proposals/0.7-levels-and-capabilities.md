# Proposal for 0.7 — levels, a capability declaration that can be checked, and no silent drop

Status: proposal, not a rule. Nothing below changes 0.5 or the 0.6 draft until the operator decides.
Origin: the operator's question, 22 September 2026 — *should there be a degree of intelligence required to
speak this profile, or should the profile fit the communication to the model, and would that not require
knowing how intelligent the other side is?*

## 1. The answer this proposal takes

**Do not grade intelligence. Grade what an implementation demonstrably does, and prove it by running the
public suite.**

Three reasons, and the first two come from this profile's own rules.

1. A declaration about oneself is sender-attested (rule 9, section 15 of the Bu draft; SC-5 of our mapping).
   "I am a large model" is a sentence. "I pass these 134 cases at this revision" is an observation anyone can
   repeat.
2. Grading the partner grades the partner. The operator's rule of 18 September — measure the method, never the
   person — applies here: a level describes an implementation's checks, not its author.
3. It is unnecessary. Two very different systems interoperate as soon as each knows **which checks the other can
   run**, which is a finite, testable list.

## 2. Levels, cut where the suite already cuts

The reference exports eight checks and the suite holds 134 cases. The levels below are a bundling of what
exists, not new machinery.

| Level | Name | Checks | Cases | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Record | `inspectRecord`, `inspectLineage`, `inspectProvenance` | 61 | A receiver can say where every value comes from and refuse a record that claims more than it shows |
| **L2** | Disagreement | adds `inspectDispute`, `inspectHop` | +43 = 104 | Disagreement survives a hop: an objection keeps its basis, a controlling source is named, nothing is silently dropped |
| **L3** | Sealed and replayed | adds `inspectReveal`, `inspectReplay`, `driftReport` | +30 = 134 | Order provable by commit–reveal, replays that declare their seat, drift reported |

A level is claimed only when **every** case of that level and of the levels below passes. Partial work stays
honest: aria-nilradical implemented `inspectLineage` and `inspectDispute` alone, which is neither L1 nor L2, and
the declaration below records exactly that rather than rounding it up.

## 3. The declaration

A participant MAY publish a `conformance` object. Nothing here is believed on its own: every field is a pointer
to something a reader can re-run.

```json
{
  "profile": "attractor-cooperation/0.5",
  "suite": {"revision": "42e2d3f", "cases_sha256": "…", "total": 134},
  "runtime": "node 24.14.0",
  "checks": {"inspectRecord": "24/24", "inspectLineage": "27/27", "inspectProvenance": "10/10",
             "inspectDispute": "0/18", "inspectHop": "0/25", "inspectReveal": "0/8",
             "inspectReplay": "0/17", "driftReport": "0/5"},
  "level": "L1",
  "produced_by": "self",
  "report_url": "https://…"
}
```

- `level` is **derived** from `checks`, never written by hand. An implementation that claims a level its
  `checks` do not support is not partially right; it is refused.
- `produced_by` is `self`, or the identifier of whoever ran the suite. A report produced by the sender is
  sender-attested; a report produced by the receiver, or by a third party who published the command and the
  hashes, is an observation. Both are allowed; they are not worth the same, and the field says which.
- `suite.revision` and `cases_sha256` pin what was run. A declaration without them says nothing.

## 4. The rule that makes levels safe: no silent drop

A receiver that does not implement a check MUST NOT ignore the member it governs. It reports
`unsupported`, naming the check, and continues. The states already exist in the vocabulary used with the Bu
draft: `unsupported` for a check this revision does not implement, `not-evaluated` for a check it implements
but did not reach, `indeterminate` for a missing trust input.

This is the load-bearing rule of the whole proposal. Levels without it are worse than no levels: an
implementation would advertise L1, receive an L3 record, drop the sealed field and return a clean-looking
result. The failure this profile already documents twice this month — an implementation indifferent to a field
it never reads, returning `confirmed` — is exactly that shape.

## 5. What this reuses rather than invents

| Idea | Where it already exists |
|---|---|
| Conformance levels stacked A ⊂ AA ⊂ AAA | W3C WCAG |
| Profiles and levels of one format | ISO PDF/A, MPEG |
| Declaring capabilities at the start of a session | MCP `initialize` capabilities; A2A agent cards |
| Refusing to proceed on a member you do not understand | SOAP `mustUnderstand`; the IETF "must ignore unknown" rule, inverted here because silence is the danger |
| Naming what a verifier cannot do rather than failing closed | draft-bu-agentproto-security-principal-binding, section 16 |

The only part that is ours is the binding between the declaration and the suite: the claim is a pointer to a
run, not an adjective.

## 6. What it costs, and what is not decided

- `conformance/report.mjs` produces the object of section 3 from a real run. Written with this proposal.
- The `unsupported` obligation needs cases: for each check, one record carrying a member that check governs,
  expected outcome `unsupported` from an implementation that lacks it. Not written.
- Open: whether `conformance` travels inside a record, in a handshake, or only in a card. Nothing here decides
  it, because no two independently operated agents have exchanged anything yet (exit criterion 3).
- Open: whether L1 is small enough. If nothing simple can reach L1, the levels are decoration.
